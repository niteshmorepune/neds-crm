Words we use differently than you'd guess.

These sound interchangeable but aren't — picking the wrong one makes a record invisible to whatever report or reminder keys off it.

Lead → Client

A Lead is someone who hasn't paid yet — an inquiry you're still working. A Client (called "Customer" in the code) is a real company with at least one won Deal. A Lead doesn't become a Client by editing a field — it becomes one when you actually hit Convert.

Lead status: New → Contacted → Qualified → Converted (or Lost)

Qualified means you've confirmed a real budget and a real need — not just that you spoke to them again. Converted means this lead actually became a Deal and a Client record, via the Convert button — never set by hand as a label with nothing behind it.

Deal stages: New → Contacted → Proposal → Negotiation → Won / Lost

A Deal is one specific piece of work being sold, priced in ₹. Negotiation specifically means a quotation has actually been sent and you're discussing terms — don't drag a card there just because you talked to the client again; send the quotation first.

Deal vs Quotation

The Deal is the pipeline card — stage, value, service, owner. The Quotation is the actual priced document you send the client, generated from the Deal and downloadable as a PDF. A Deal sitting in Negotiation or later should always have a real Quotation attached to it — if it doesn't, something's out of sync and needs fixing before it moves further.

Service

The category tag on a Lead, Deal, Project, or Quotation line — SEO, GMB, Website Design & Development, Social Media Management, Performance Marketing, Software Development, AI Automation, AMC Service, plus anything an admin has added since. Pick whichever is the closest real match. If one deal genuinely spans two services, put the main one here and name the second in the title.
Don't invent a catch-all Every report that slices pipeline value "by service" reads this one field. A vague catch-all service quietly hides real SEO or Social Media revenue from those reports — if nothing fits cleanly, ask before adding a new service rather than picking the closest-sounding existing one that isn't actually it.

Project vs Task ("Emptask" in the sidebar)

A Project is created once a Deal is Won — it's where a client's ongoing delivery work lives. A Task is one specific unit of work inside a Project (or standalone) — what actually gets assigned to a person, tracked, and marked done. Deals and Leads are never "worked" directly; the Tasks under a Project are.

Giving someone the right role

For Admin/Manager: how to map a real job title onto the CRM's access levels — and where that mapping actually stops.

The one thing to understand first

The CRM doesn't have a field for "Graphic Designer." It has exactly seven role values — Admin, Manager, Sales, Support, Accounts, Intern, Telecaller — and every permission in the system (what someone can see, edit, approve, or send) comes from one of those seven, not from their actual job title.
So hiring a Website Designer or a Performance Marketing Executive isn't a matter of finding "their" role — it's picking whichever of the seven gives them the closest real access, then trimming what shows in their sidebar. The table below is that mapping, done once, for the titles you listed.

Two ways to narrow what someone sees — and one honest limit

Additional Roles (Users → edit person → Additional Roles) — for someone who genuinely does two jobs, not just one with a fancy title. Example: a Performance Marketing Executive who also qualifies inbound leads by phone gets primary role Support + additional role Sales — they keep the Support-routed project work and gain Leads/Deals/Calling on top, without you re-deciding their whole access level.
Menu Controller (Admin → Menu Controller, or per-user on their own Users row) — hides or shows a sidebar link, per role or per person. Good for tidying a cluttered sidebar so someone only sees what's relevant to them.
The limit Menu Controller doesn't cover Hiding a sidebar item only hides the link — it does not take away the underlying permission. This is exactly why a role's real access always has to be checked against what the role itself allows, not what's merely visible: giving someone the Sales role and hiding Quotations/Invoices from their sidebar would not have stopped them creating one, since Sales' policy grants that unconditionally, independent of the menu. That real gap is what the Telecaller role now exists to close — a genuinely restricted role, not Sales-with-hidden-links. If a similar distinction ever seems to matter for another role, it needs an actual code change, never a Menu Controller setting alone.

Step by step, for a new hire

  1. Users → New User — name, email, phone.
  2. Set their Primary Role using the table above.
  3. Only tick an Additional Role if they truly straddle two functions — not by default.
  4. Save. They can log in and check in from day one.
  5. (Optional) Menu Controller → per-role default, or their own Users row → per-item Default / Show / Hide, to tidy their sidebar. Remember: cosmetic, not a security boundary.
  6. If they're joining a Project, add them to that project's Assignees (Lead or Member) yourself — that's what actually routes onboarding and recurring tasks to them, not their CRM role alone.
